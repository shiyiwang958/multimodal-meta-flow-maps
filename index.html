Overview

Biological design is continuous (molecular structures), discrete (DNA residue types), and often both at once (protein structure–sequence co-design). Diffusion and CTMC-based methods handle multimodal generation, but adapting them at inference time to satisfy a design constraint remains hard.

We adapt Meta Flow Maps to biological domains, developing the framework for discrete (dMFM) and multimodal (multiMFM) settings. One reward-independent flow map is distilled per base generator; any differentiable objective can then be applied at sampling time without retraining anything. We demonstrate it on DNA sequence design with a target mechanical property, and on molecular property design with QM9 and GEOM-Drugs.

Motivation

Molecular and sequence design rarely has one objective. A project wants validity, then binding affinity, then solubility, then some constraint nobody anticipated. Training a conditional generator for each specification makes experimentation slow.

Steering a fixed generator with a reward avoids that, and its accuracy comes down to one quantity: the value function \(V_t\), the expected reward of the finished object given a noisy intermediate state. Estimating it well means sampling the posterior over clean outputs consistent with that state. Existing methods either approximate the posterior with a point estimate, which is biased and cannot be improved by spending more compute, or simulate trajectories, which is expensive.

  • multiMFM steers 3D molecular generation toward property targets with a single distilled map reused across objectives.
  • We formulate discrete GLASS posterior sampling and derive the discrete Meta Flow Map.
  • Combining the continuous and discrete maps gives mixed discrete–continuous steering.
  • dMFM optimizes generated DNA sequences toward prescribed cyclizability values.

The key idea

A Meta Flow Map is a one-step sampler that takes Gaussian noise to a draw from the posterior \(p_{1|t}(\cdot \mid x_t)\). Drawing \(N\) noises gives \(N\) look-aheads at the finished object, and so a Monte Carlo estimate of the reward gradient that steers the trajectory:

\[ \widehat{\nabla V}^{\,r}_t(x_t) = \nabla_x \log\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\exp\big(r(\Phi(\epsilon_i;t,x_t))\big)\right) \]

Unlike a point estimate, this converges as \(N\) grows. We derive the discrete analogue by extending GLASS to categorical data on the simplex, where the map is distilled against the base denoiser, and combine the two into a joint map over \(x_t = (y_t, a_t)\) that steers coordinates and atom types with partial gradients of the same value function.

Results

  • QM9, six properties 5 of 6 best reported MAE among steering methods, from one backbone rather than one generator per property.
  • Value estimate ~100× lower error than the point estimates it replaces, and it keeps falling with budget.
  • DNA, oracle MAE 0.15 from 1.17 against a parent-disjoint oracle; 98% of paired samples improve.

DNA designed for a target mechanical property

Intrinsic cyclizability, a proxy for DNA bendability, is sequence-encoded and carries regulatory information beyond motif content. We distill a dMFM from a base discrete flow model and steer 50-bp sequences toward a prescribed predicted value, guided by one regressor and evaluated by a second trained on disjoint parent sequences.

Guidance cuts oracle error from 1.174 to 0.153 at target \(y^\star{=}{+}1\), and from 0.833 to 0.117 at \(y^\star{=}{-}1\). Because the evaluator never saw the guide's training parents, the gain is not an artifact of scoring with the model being optimized. The guided errors sit near the oracle's own accuracy against measured values, so this shows guidance reaching the target region rather than resolving finer differences.

Molecular property guidance

For molecules the discrete modality is atom types and the continuous modality is coordinates. multiMFM beats the published TFG-Flow values on every property, and attains the best reported error on five of six. On top of it, steer-search keeps the best look-ahead the estimator has already computed instead of discarding it, which improves accuracy and validity at exactly zero additional generative-model calls.

The two panels below separate the method's premise from its consequence. A flow map makes the value function reachable by Monte Carlo, where a denoiser can only approximate it — and the estimator that recovers it is the one that steers best per network call.

One correction worth noting: implemented faithfully and gated on effective sample size, Feynman–Kac steering reduces to best-of-\(N\) here, as does MCTS on DNA, where the reward is terminal-only. The figure shows them superimposed rather than as distinct baselines.

Scaling to drug-sized molecules

GEOM-Drugs reaches 71 heavy atoms against 9 for QM9. On the structural fingerprint task we differentiate a relaxation of the reported Tanimoto metric rather than a squared error on the same features, which is what separates a result from a null here. The metric needs a floor before it can be read: two unrelated real molecules already score about 0.22, because a path-based fingerprint rewards genericness.

Guidance clears the floor by several times what the unguided generator manages, over three replicates of 4,096 molecules, and PoseBusters validity rises at the same time, so the gain is not bought with structural quality.
